How blocking works

Useful friction, explained plainly.

Site blocking is optional and off until you turn it on. Here is exactly what it does, what it can't do, and why it isn't a lock.

The short version

Before a browser or app can open a site, your phone looks up the site's name: it asks a DNS server "where is this name?" When you turn on site blocking, Redirect starts a small VPN that runs entirely on your phone and sees those lookups. If the name is on the blocklist, the lookup is refused on the phone and the site can't be reached. If it isn't, the lookup goes on to your phone's usual DNS, just as it would without Redirect.

  1. You open a sitein a browser or an app.
  2. Your phone asks for its addressa lookup of the site's name.
  3. Redirect checks the nameagainst the list, on your phone.
  4. On the list: refusedThe site can't be reached. Not on it: the lookup goes on to your phone's usual DNS.

When a site is blocked, Redirect can offer a pause: a quiet notification that never names the site, or the pause card if app pauses are on. You can turn the notification off.

What's on the list

  • The standard list: the community-maintained OISD nsfw-small list of known adult sites (the bundled July 2026 snapshot has about 18,000 entries), plus a small list Redirect adds to it.
  • Allowed sites: a site you allow is let through, even if it is on the standard list.
  • Updates only when you ask: nothing refreshes by itself. If you tap Update now, Redirect downloads the latest OISD list over HTTPS from nsfw-small.oisd.nl. That host can see ordinary request details such as your IP address and the time; the request contains none of your Redirect data.

What it sees, and what it doesn't

Matching happens on your phone. Redirect does not proxy page content, and it does not look at images, video, or what's on a page. It has no server of its own, so the names your phone looks up are never sent to Redirect. Lookups that aren't blocked are handed to Android's own resolver on your normal connection, so your Android DNS settings apply. Site blocking doesn't run while Private DNS is set to a named provider. Redirect opens no DNS connection of its own and has no fallback resolver: if there is no connection, the lookup simply fails. It keeps no record of the names your phone looks up. Your network's resolver can see those lookups under their own terms, as it could without Redirect. They may travel unencrypted, so anyone watching the network can read them too.

What gets around it

Site blocking is useful friction, not a wall. These all bypass it:

  • Private DNS set to a named provider in Android's settings. Site blocking can't run then; it waits until Private DNS is set to Automatic or Off.
  • Secure DNS in a browser set to a custom provider. In Chrome, keep Settings › Privacy and security › Use secure DNS on "current service provider". Redirect can't see or test another app's Secure DNS setting.
  • Apps with their own resolver, IPv6 DNS, and DNS-over-TCP.
  • Another VPN. Android allows one active VPN at a time.
  • A site you opened just before you blocked it may keep working for a few minutes, and a site can have other addresses.

To check it is working, open Protection › Test site blocking: it verifies the tunnel, the blocklist, and the local filter.

Why you can turn it off

Redirect is built for an adult who chose it. It is not tamper-proof software and it is not a lock someone else holds. Every guardrail is off until you turn it on, and you decide how much friction to add:

  • Gentle (the default): turning it off takes effect straight away. On an app pause, “Open anyway” counts down and can be used after 10 seconds.
  • Committed: changes that lower protection wait for the delay you chose: 15 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours or 3 days. App pauses don't offer “Open anyway”.
  • Anchor: follows the same waiting period as Committed, and app pauses don't offer “Open anyway” either.

Uninstalling always removes it. That is deliberate: Redirect is friction you chose, never a trap.

In plain words: Redirect blocks known adult sites on this phone, while it can see the lookup. It can't block everything, and it isn't parental control.

The other guardrail

App pauses

For apps you choose, Redirect can notice which app is in the foreground and place your pause card over it, using Android Accessibility. It reads only the app's package name. It does not read screen text, take screenshots, type, or perform gestures, and the pause can be passed through.

The Accessibility service can be switched off in Android Settings. More detail is in the privacy policy.
