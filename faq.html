Questions before you install.

What people ask about a blocker before they trust it. Email us if yours isn't here.

  • Do I need an account?

    No. There is no Redirect account, no sign-in, and no email address to give. Every feature works without one, and nothing is synced to a cloud.

  • What leaves my phone?

    Your journal, check-ins and plans stay on your phone unless you export or share them. Redirect has no analytics, no ads, no crash-reporting service and no server of its own. Information can leave the phone in three ways. Redirect does the first two itself, and only when you've turned something on:

    • While site blocking is on, lookups that aren't blocked go to Android's own resolver, so they reach your network's DNS, as they would without Redirect. Site blocking doesn't run while Private DNS is set to a named provider.
    • If you tap Update now, the blocklist is downloaded from the OISD host.
    • Files you export or share yourself, such as an export of your records.

    The privacy policy has the full detail.

  • What does the VPN see?

    The VPN runs on your phone and only looks at lookups: the names of sites your phone is trying to reach. It compares each name with the blocklist on the phone. It does not carry or read page content, images, or video, and it sends nothing to Redirect. Android shows a VPN indicator while it is on. How blocking works

  • Does Redirect send reports to anyone?

    No. There is no partner dashboard, no automatic report and no login for anyone else. Nobody is told when you change a setting or remove the app. You choose whether to share a message, an export or a diagnostic report. An optional app lock uses your phone's own screen lock to keep the journal, progress, and plans private on a shared or borrowed phone; the pause is never locked. Someone holding your unlocked phone may still see what's on it.

  • Does it work with Private DNS or Secure DNS?

    Not with a named provider. While Android's Private DNS is set to a named provider, site blocking can't run; it waits until Private DNS is set to Automatic or Off. A custom Secure DNS provider in a browser sends lookups around Redirect, so site blocking can't see them. In Chrome, keep Use secure DNS on "current service provider". Apps with their own resolver, IPv6 DNS, DNS-over-TCP, and other VPNs bypass it too.

  • Can I turn it off?

    Yes. Every guardrail is off until you turn it on. On the default level it turns off straight away; a stricter level waits for the delay you chose. Uninstalling always removes it. Redirect isn't tamper-proof, by design.

  • Will it block everything?

    No. It blocks known adult sites, using the community-maintained OISD list, while your phone's lookups go through it. New or unlisted sites, content inside other apps, and the bypasses above can all get through. It is one layer of friction.

  • Can I use another VPN at the same time?

    Not with site blocking on. Android allows one active VPN at a time, so site blocking and another VPN can't run together. The pause, your plans and App pauses don't use the VPN and keep working.

  • Does Redirect look at my screen or my photos?

    It never looks at photos, images, or video, and it never takes screenshots. App pauses are optional, and see only which app is open. Redirect doesn't ask for camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos, or SMS access.

  • Is it free?

    Yes. Redirect is free, with no ads and nothing to buy in the app.

  • I lost my phone. Is everything gone?

    Redirect has no cloud backup, and Android's own app backup is switched off on purpose. If the phone is lost or wiped, Redirect can't recover your records. That is the price of having nothing stored anywhere else. You can keep an export, a readable copy of your records, somewhere safe, but it can't be restored into the app.

  • Is this treatment?

    No. Redirect is a self-support tool for the adult using it. It isn't medical treatment, therapy, or crisis support, and it can work alongside them. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please contact your local emergency number or a crisis line where you live.
